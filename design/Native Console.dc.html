1Foundations — the cyan
1aThe cyan — and why this one rather than the obvious one.

Deep cyan, hue held at 193°

Cyan goes wrong for one measurable reason: at full chroma it is a light colour, so a white label on it fails contrast and the whole thing reads cheap. The fix isn't a duller cyan — it's splitting the family in two. The deep end carries fills and text; the vibrant end lives on tints and dark surfaces where it has room to be bright.

White on #06B6D4 is 2.4:1. The label floats rather than sits, so the button reads like a placeholder — that's the "trash cyan" feeling, and it's a luminance problem, not a taste problem.

Ours
Create account

White on #0B7F99 is 4.7:1 — it clears AA at body size, so the fill can actually behave like a fill. Same hue family, just enough depth to hold a word.

600 fills, 700 speaks
#0B7F99 for buttons and solid fills. #095F73 for text and links — 7.3:1 on white. 500 is for icons, charts and strokes only; at 3.5:1 it is not a text colour.
193°, not 180°
Pure 180° aqua is the shade that reads as swimming pool. Nudging toward blue keeps it unmistakably cyan while letting it sit next to the ink ramp without vibrating.
Green is freed
Today brand and profit are the same green, so a Pay button and a profit figure look identical. Moving the brand to cyan lets green mean profit and nothing else — a real gain, not just a repaint.

4cBreakpoint rules — what actually changes, and where.
Width
Layout
≥ 1180
Landscape tablet, till
4-column menu. 1a keeps the dock and gains a persistent right cart column; 1b keeps tabs and rail and shows seat cards two-up. Secondary controls may drop to 36px.
768 – 1179
Portrait tablet — 3a / 3b
3-column menu. The two directions are fully themselves here: dock vs tabs, sheet vs inline modifiers. This is the only band where the choice materially matters.
560 – 767
Small tablet, phone landscape
2-column menu. Category rail rotates to a chip row; bill tabs become the selector button; the dock drops to the peek bar. Both directions now look the same except at payment.
< 560
Phone — 4a / 4b
2-column menu at a 156px tile floor. Modifiers become a full-height page. Split cards stack. Every target holds at 44px — nothing shrinks to fit.
Three components carry the whole responsive story
Bill switcher
Tabs → chip strip → selector button. Same data, three densities.
Cart
Three states — docked, peek bar, full sheet. Width picks the resting state; the user can always reach the sheet.
Menu grid
Auto-fill on a 156px minimum, so column count falls out of width instead of being hard-coded per breakpoint.

Practical consequence: pick the direction on tablet ergonomics alone — whichever you choose, the phone build is the same shell, so there's no extra cost either way.
